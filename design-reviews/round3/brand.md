# Brand and visual identity review: Mizan (v3)

## Score: 73/100 (round 2: 68, round 1: 61)
This is now strong professional identity work with a recognisable signature: the arched window, lapis ink, the khatam lattice and the `§` margin column. What holds it below 80 is the 3D craft inside that window, a few polish regressions, and a document grid that is applied to some sections and not others.

## What works
- **The arched window is the identity's best device.** The hero 3D now sits inside a pointed-arch frame with a lapis inner rule (`desktop/00-y0.png`). The same arch frames the static scale emblem (`desktop-no3d/00-y0.png`) and the new static sunset (`desktop-no3d/04-y3240.png`, `mobile/05-y3798.png`). One shape carries the brand across 3D, still and mobile, which is what a real identity system does. It also fixes the v2 hero problems: no ghost bleed, no cropped pan, and a caption anchored under the frame.
- **The 3D vocabulary is finally coherent.** Minarets and crenellations are gone. The skyline is low blocks with small arched openings. The lattice screens cast star-shaped light onto the road (`desktop/03-y2430.png`, `04-y3240.png`), a genuinely lovely, ownable moment. The active gate is drawn in lapis with a brass khatam step number (`desktop/03-y2430.png`), which ties the scene to the page's colour system.
- **A layout signature has arrived.** §4, §5 and "Next steps" use a margin column: a large serif `§` numeral, a vertical hairline, and content to its right (`desktop/08-y6480.png`, `09-y7290.png`, `12-y9720.png`). It reads like a bound register or legal instrument and gives the long document sections structure without decoration.
- **The static sunset is now a real illustration.** The arch window holds a lapis skyline silhouette and a brass sun (`desktop-no3d/04-y3240.png`). It is restrained, on-palette and on-motif, and it gives phones a version of the brand's signature idea.
- **Palette discipline holds and extends.** The code block is now lapis-ink instead of a generic black (`desktop/09-y7290.png`). The `§` numerals, seal, gate, verse and buttons all share lapis, brass stays on the seal, sun and khatam badge, and statuses stay separate.

## Problems

- **High: the scene inside the hero window is muddy.** The best frame of the brand is filled with a grey-brown haze. The scale is mid-bronze on a grey-brown plinth against grey-brown walls, and the lower third fades to flat grey (`desktop/00-y0.png`). The window promises a crisp illustration; what is inside reads as an underlit render. Compare it with the static arch next to it in `desktop-no3d/00-y0.png`, which is crisper and more confident.
- **High: the 3D world is still mostly untextured boxes outside the hero objects.** The lattice screens and lapis gate are good, but the walls and buildings are flat Lambert slabs in olive, mud and taupe (`desktop/03-y2430.png` left third, `04-y3240.png`, `05-y4050.png`). The olive green walls in particular are off-palette. The hourglass is still the weakest object: grey smoky glass by day (`desktop/06-y4860.png`) and a ghostly blob at night (`desktop/07-y5670.png`). The "cut paper" claim is true of perhaps 20% of the scene.
- **Med (regression): the register's Record column is crushed.** The product names now wrap to three or four lines ("Sample / Token A / v1.2.0", and "Sample / Bridge D / March / 2026 / release"), while Validity and Board conclusion have far more room than they need (`desktop/01-y810.png`, `02-y1620.png`). In v2 these fit on one or two lines. The register is the product's core artefact, and it now looks broken at 1440px.
- **Med: the grid is applied inconsistently.** The margin `§` column appears on §4, §5 and Next steps, and on §3 in static mode. But the register (§1) is still a full-width card with a small inline `§1`, and the 3D §2 and §3 panels use the old small inline `§2`/`§3` (`desktop/01-y810.png`, `03-y2430.png`, `06-y4860.png`). There are two numbering systems on one page. It should be one bound document.
- **Med: the code block clips its content.** In the Solidity tab, lines are cut mid-token at the right edge ("uint64 asO", "recordId", "IMizanOracle.Ru") with no fade or scroll cue (`desktop/09-y7290.png`, `10-y8100.png`). The narrower column introduced by the margin grid caused this. In a developer section, clipped code reads as a bug.
- **Med: scene exits are still a soft fog.** The night scene dissolves into paper through a grey gradient band behind the bottom of the §3 panel (`desktop/07-y5670.png`), and the road rises out of haze under the register (`desktop/02-y1620.png`). The hero proved a hard arched edge works; the exits should use the same treatment.
- **Low: the timeline labels still collide.** "Ends 12 Oct 2027" and "Feb 2028" overlap at different baselines on the 3D §3 panel (`desktop/06-y4860.png`, `07-y5670.png`). This was flagged in round 2.
- **Low: on mobile the sunset illustration is orphaned.** It sits above the §3 card, with the section break between them, so it appears before its heading and reads as belonging to §2 (`mobile/05-y3798.png`, `06-y4558.png`). The section strip still clips "Who d…", though it now fades.
- **Low: the typography is still a single-superfamily safe choice.** Plex Serif plus Plex Sans works, and the margin numerals at large size look good, but nothing in the type itself is ownable. The identity rests on the arch, the lattice, lapis and the Kufi mark. That is enough for the mid-70s, not for the 80s.

## Top 5 recommendations (ranked by score impact)

1. **Relight and recolour the hero window scene.** Raise the key light and add a warm rim on the scale. Make the plinth paper-white or lapis so the scale separates. Replace the grey fog at the bottom of the window with a clean paper floor, and push the background walls lighter and lower in contrast, so the window reads as a crisp illustrated vignette like the static arch beside it. This is the first frame and the brand's showpiece.
2. **Finish the cut-paper world.** Remove the olive walls. Give every building the lattice treatment (bevelled paper with a light edge and a star or arch cutout) in two or three paper tones plus lapis. Replace the glass hourglass with a cut-paper silhouette hourglass whose sand is a brass fill, so it reads at both day and night.
3. **Apply one grid everywhere.** Put the large margin `§` numeral and hairline on §1 (register), and on §2 and §3 in 3D mode: place the numeral in the margin outside the floating panel, or move the panels into the grid. Remove the small inline `§` style entirely.
4. **Fix the three polish regressions.** Give the register's Record column a minimum width of about 180px, let Validity shrink, and keep names on one line. Make the code block scroll horizontally with a right-edge fade, or wrap long lines with a hanging indent. Separate the "Ends" and "Feb 2028" tick labels onto one baseline, or drop "Feb 2028".
5. **Close each 3D section with a hard edge.** Use the arch silhouette, or a straight paper edge with a soft cast shadow, where the scene begins under the register and where it ends after §3. Move the mobile sunset illustration inside or directly under the §3 heading.

## What would get this to 85+
The system is now there: arch window, lapis, khatam lattice and a margin `§` grid. What separates this from 85 is execution, not concept. The hero window needs to contain a crisp, well-lit vignette rather than haze, and the rest of the valley needs to be built in the same cut-paper language as the lattice screens, with a paper-silhouette hourglass. The margin grid then needs to run through every section, including the register, with the register table's column regression and the clipped code fixed. Do that, and the page would hold up as a distinctive studio piece.
