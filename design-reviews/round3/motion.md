# Motion and interaction review: Mizan (round 3)

**Score: 80/100.** The scroll story now has real pacing: each gate gets a hold, only the active gate shows its number, the hero sits inside an arch window, and the still page tells the same story in SVG. What is left is framing: the gate number is cropped during the hold, the hero window empties as you scroll, and the 3D cut-off for short screens is too aggressive.

## What works

- **Pacing is fixed, with a hold on each gate.** The tracks are back to 260vh and 190vh (`global.css:176-177`). I measured **1440 px for five steps (288 px per step)** and **810 px for the 460-day year**, more than double round 2. `howStation` now uses a three-part curve per step (`world.ts:457-466`): it approaches over 30% of the step, holds 14 units in front of the arch for 40%, then passes through over the last 30%. A wheel notch can no longer skip a gate, and the camera rests while the reader reads. This is the right fix for the problem rather than just a bigger number.
- **The number badge now belongs to the active gate.** Only the active gate shows its badge (`world.ts:629`), and the badge hangs inside the arch opening (`world.ts:352-353`). In `desktop/03-y2430.png` the panel says "1 Intake" and the scene shows a lapis arch with a "1" badge framed in it. The round 2 "next step's badge" mismatch is gone.
- **The hero arch window is a strong idea.** A paper SVG mask with a pointed-arch cut-out (`HeroWindow.tsx`) frames the fixed canvas, so the 3D reads as "looking through a window" and fits the brand. The line-art poster sits inside the same arch until `stage-ready` and fades in place (`global.css:157-159`). Poster and scene now share one frame, which fixes round 2's cross-dissolve between two different objects. The scale is no longer cropped at 1440 or 1280 (`desktop/00-y0.png`, `scratchpad/shots-tool/r3-1280.png`).
- **The still page now carries the metaphor.** In static mode §3 shows an arch-framed sky with a sun that tracks the slider and a town that goes to night after expiry (`Art.tsx` `SkyArt`, `desktop-reduced/04-y3240.png`, `mobile/05-y3798.png`). Reduced-motion, no-WebGL and phone readers get the same idea, "sunset means expired", without motion. The empty right column on desktop static is filled.
- **The night beat reads.** The sand gets a warm emissive after expiry (`world.ts:598`), so the empty top bulb and the full bottom bulb stand out against the dark town (`desktop/07-y5670.png`, `scratchpad/shots-tool/r3-night.png`). Shadows moved to 2048² PCFSoft (`world.ts:72,155`). The lattice screens cast soft, legible star patterns (`desktop/03`, `04`), with no stair-stepping in the stills I checked.
- **The render loop is still disciplined.** I measured 0 draws when idle and about 76 draws per pass while settling. The covered-section skip and the snap on large jumps are still in place.

## Problems

- **Med: The gate number is cropped during the hold.** At the 14-unit hold, the arch fills the frame and the badge at y=8.1 sits under the nav bar. Mid-step frames show only the bottom half of the star: "2" in `scratchpad/shots-tool/r3-how-0.3.png` and "5" in `r3-how-0.9.png`. The badge is fully visible only during the approach (`desktop/03-y2430.png`). The hold is the 40% of each step the reader spends longest on, so the anchor that ties the panel to the scene is clipped exactly then.
- **Med: For about 30% of every step, no active gate is in view.** In the pass-through phase the camera has gone through the lapis arch while the next arch is still paper and unnumbered. `desktop/04-y3240.png` shows the panel on "3 Screens" with no lapis arch and no badge anywhere. It is brief, but it is a regular dead zone in a scene whose job is to show where you are.
- **Med: The hero window empties as you scroll.** The arch mask scrolls with the hero, but the canvas is fixed and the camera only dollies 20%. As the window rises, it frames empty sky and the scale drops out of the bottom (`scratchpad/shots-tool/r3-y450.png`: a beige rectangle with a minaret tip). The parallax idea is good, but the subject should stay in the window. Either keep the scale centred in the moving window or fade the window out before it frames nothing.
- **Med (worse than round 2): The short-screen cut-off now hits ordinary laptops.** `compact` changed from `max-height: 520px` to `max-height: 680px` (`App.tsx:28`). A 1366×768 laptop has a viewport of about 650–670 px with browser chrome, so it gets the still page. So do many 13-inch laptops with a bookmarks bar, and any desktop window that isn't maximised. The still page is good, but a common desktop setup loses the main visual. Measure the panel against `innerHeight` instead, for example by using 3D whenever the tallest sticky panel plus the nav fits.
- **Low: The card still resizes a little and has no exit animation.** Remounting `step-body` with `key={step}` plays `stepIn` on the new step only (`global.css:188-189`). The old step disappears instantly, and the card height changes by up to 24 px between steps (measured 378–402 px). It is far better than round 2's 160 px of dead space, but the bottom edge still jumps slightly on every other step.
- **Low: The hero scene through the arch is muddy.** The bottom 40% of the arch is a flat grey-brown ground with a dark lamp post in front of the plinth (`desktop/00-y0.png`). At 1280 a hard vertical edge of hill geometry shows at the arch's left side (`r3-1280.png`, x≈735). The poster line art looks cleaner than the render it hands over to.

## Top 5 recommendations (ranked by score impact)

1. **Frame the hold on the badge.** Pull the hold back to about 20 units (`HOLD ≈ 0.43`), lower the camera's look-at so the apex and badge sit about 25% below the top edge, or move the badge to `y ≈ 6.8` inside the opening. Check the five mid-step frames at 1440×900 and 1280×720: the full star must clear the nav bar by at least 24 px.
2. **Close the pass-through dead zone.** Hand the lapis colour and badge to the next gate at the start of the pass-through (t > 0.7) rather than at the step boundary, or start the next gate's colour fade during the last 30% with a matching early cross-fade of the panel. Either way, one lapis, numbered arch should be in frame at every scroll position in §2.
3. **Keep the subject in the hero window.** Drive `VIEW.hero[1]` (vertical view offset) from the arch's on-screen y position so the scale follows the window up as the hero scrolls. Alternatively, fade the arch window and caption to the plain paper hero over the first 40% of the scroll. Retune the hero camera so the ground isn't 40% of the arch, and push the left hill cards out of the arch's field of view.
4. **Loosen the compact rule.** Replace `max-height: 680px` with a measured check: use 3D when `innerHeight ≥ nav + tallest panel + 40px` (about 560 px today). Restore 3D for 1366×768 laptops and confirm the sticky panels still fit.
5. **Add an exit to the step swap and fix the card height.** Keep the outgoing step mounted for 220 ms with a fade-out (or use a two-slot cross-fade) so the change reads as one move. Set `min-height` on `.step-body` to the tallest step (402 px) so the card edge is stable without dead space.

## What would get this to 85+

The structure, pacing and fallbacks are now at a professional level. What is left is precision. At every scroll position, exactly one numbered lapis arch should be fully in frame, the hero subject should stay in its window as it moves, and common laptop sizes should get the 3D. Fix those three framing issues and smooth the last step-swap detail, and this becomes a coherent piece of scroll storytelling where every motion carries meaning.
