# Motion and interaction review: Mizan (round 2)

**Score: 75/100.** This round fixed the joins: the seams are gone, the step text cross-fades, the hero shows a poster image until the 3D is ready, shadows no longer crawl and draw calls roughly halved. The scroll tracks got much shorter, though, so the walk through the gates now races past, and the lit gate's own badge is never in view.

## What works

- **The seams are fixed properly.** The register and the first document section now fade into and out of the canvas with 22vh paper gradients (`global.css:228-231`). The register lifts away into the valley (`desktop/02-y1620.png`) and the night scene fades back into paper (`desktop/05-y4050.png` bottom, `desktop/06-y4860.png` top). There are no hard horizontal cuts anywhere in the 3D run.
- **The register hides part of the camera move.** The hero gets a slow 20% dolly while you read (`world.ts:481-485`). The other 80% happens while the opaque register covers the canvas, so §2 opens exactly on the first arch. `covered()` skips every draw while the register fills the screen (I measured 0 draw calls while scrolling 30 px under it), and `first = true` makes the camera snap when the scene reappears rather than fly (`world.ts:510-515`, `557-562`).
- **The panel and the scene now move together.** All five step bodies sit in one grid cell and cross-fade with a 6 px rise over 220 ms (`global.css:183-186`), which matches the camera's ~220 ms time constant. The step buttons also transition. The gate turns lapis quickly (`dt*9`) and snaps on jumps.
- **The hero is more expressive and loads better.** The scale now has four states: level for a current approval, a slight dip for a lapsed one, and a full tip for retracted or not approved (`world.ts:344-345`, `scratchpad/shots-tool/r2-hero-Notapproved.png`). The SVG scale acts as a poster until `stage-ready`, then fades out (`global.css:152-155`), so the hero is never half empty.
- **Rendering is tighter.** The shadow camera snaps to whole texels in light space (`world.ts:591-598`), so shadows stop crawling during scroll. Palms and clouds are gone and lamps are instanced. I measured about 70–90 draws per pass (versus about 210 in round 1) and 0 when idle. Large jumps snap rather than lerp through hills (`world.ts:566`). The page handles `webglcontextlost` by falling back to the still page, and `canRun3D` now releases its test context.
- **The scroll story keeps its meaning.** It ends on the expired night at the hourglass and does not carry the night into a register of mostly Active records, so the round 1 contradiction is gone.

## Problems

- **High: The scroll tracks are now too short, so the walk races.** `.track-how` dropped to 170vh and `.track-time` to 140vh (`global.css:172-173`). The pinned distance is section height minus vh: I measured **630 px for all five steps (126 px per step)** and **360 px for the whole 460-day timeline**. One wheel notch (~100 px) moves the camera about 28 world units, nearly a whole gate, and about 130 days. A trackpad flick goes from dawn to night in one gesture. The meaning is still there but there is no time to take it in. Round 1 had about twice the distance; this got worse.
- **Med: The badge in view belongs to the next step.** At mid-step the lit gate fills the frame and its own number sprite is above the viewport, so the only badge visible is the next gate's. In `r2-step0.png` the panel says "1 Intake" while the scene shows a "2" badge, and in `r2-step3.png` the panel says "4 Vote" while the scene shows "5". In `desktop/03-y2430.png` ("2 Evidence") the camera has already passed the active gate, so no lapis gate is visible at all. The camera passes through gate *i* only at the end of step *i* (`howStation`, `world.ts:484-486`), so for much of each step the frame looks one step ahead.
- **Med: Dead space in the pinned card.** The fixed-height card fixes the jumping but leaves about 160 px of empty sheet for four of the five steps (`r2-step0.png`, `r2-step3.png`, and `desktop/04-y3240.png`, where the empty card then meets the §3 card scrolling up). It reads as a layout bug, not as breathing room.
- **Med: Reduced motion still gets no 3D, and phones now get none either.** `staticMode = reduceMotion || compact || !webgl` (`App.tsx:30`). The still page is coherent (`mobile/06-y4558.png`), and taking the scene off phones fixes round 1's hidden-gate problem by removing the scene. Still, the brand's main visual and its core metaphor (sunset means expired) are now missing for most mobile readers and for every reduced-motion reader. Static stills of the hero, the night and the hourglass would cost almost nothing. On desktop the static §3 also leaves the right half of the screen empty (`desktop-reduced/04-y3240.png`).
- **Low: The hero scale is cropped and the poster hand-off changes style.** At 1440 the right pan is cut by the viewport edge, which is worst when it tips (`r2-hero-Notapproved.png`, `desktop/00-y0.png`). The poster is a flat line-art scale in an arch frame (`desktop-no3d/00-y0.png`), and it fades out at a different position and in a different style from the 3D scale fading in. The hand-off is a cross-dissolve between two different objects.
- **Low: Shadows are still low-resolution.** They no longer crawl, but a 1024² PCF map over 110 units still shows stepped multi-edge penumbrae on lamp posts and arches (`r2-step0.png` bottom left, `desktop/03-y2430.png`).
- **Low: The night frame is muddy.** At the end of §3 the hourglass is a dark silhouette and its sand is barely readable (`desktop/05-y4050.png`). The object that carries "the period has run out" loses contrast exactly when it matters. A rim light or a sand emissive at night would fix it.

## Top 5 recommendations (ranked by score impact)

1. **Restore the scroll budget.** Make each pinned track at least about 90vh of scroll per beat: `.track-how` about 100vh + 5×45vh (≈325vh), and `.track-time` about 100vh + 120vh. Keep the steps evenly spaced but add a short dwell plateau to each step (for example, map p through a stepped ease so the camera holds near each gate for about 30% of the step). That keeps one wheel notch from skipping a gate.
2. **Frame the active gate, not the next one.** Shift `howStation` so the camera reaches gate *i* at the *middle* of step *i*: `z = gateZ[i] + 18` at step start, then pass through the gate at mid-step and approach gate *i+1* afterward. Alternatively, lower the number sprite to sit inside the arch's apex so it stays in frame. Hide the next gate's badge until its step begins, or show it only when it is active.
3. **Fill the card, or let it resize smoothly.** Either give each step a consistent content structure (heading, two lines, a field block, plus a small diagram or icon row) so the tallest step isn't 160 px taller, or drop the shared cell and animate the height with the FLIP technique over 220 ms. Do not keep a fixed block of empty paper.
4. **Ship stills for compact and reduced-motion readers.** Render three WebP frames at build time (hero scale, the §3 sunset, the §3 night with lit windows) and show them in static mode: the hero image, and a pair in §3 that cross-fades (or simply swaps under reduced motion) when the range slider crosses expiry. This brings back the core metaphor for most phone readers at near-zero cost and fills the empty right column on desktop static.
5. **Polish the hero and night frames.** Pull the hero station back or reduce `VIEW.hero` so both pans stay inside 1440 and 1280 with margin when tipped. Make the poster SVG match the 3D camera framing, or fade the poster only after the first rendered frame and cross-fade in place. Add a cool rim light or moonlight boost on the hourglass and an emissive tint on the sand after expiry. Raise the shadow map to 2048 on desktop, or switch to `PCFSoftShadowMap`.

## What would get this to 85+

The structure is now sound: no seams, synced text, a poster frame, a render loop that sleeps, and rendering inside budget. What is missing is pacing and framing. Give each gate and the year enough scroll to land, hold the camera on the gate the panel is describing, and remove the empty card space. Then bring the metaphor back to phone and reduced-motion readers with pre-rendered stills. With those done, this would be distinctive studio-level scroll storytelling.
