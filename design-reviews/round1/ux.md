# UX and information architecture review: Mizan (round 1)

**Score: 68/100.** The hero and the record model are clear and carefully worded, but the page is long and repeats itself. It buries its core tool behind two scroll-driven explainers, has no navigation on mobile, and the record sheet, the art and the builder spec each contradict the "status first" rule the page teaches.

## What works

- **The 5-second test passes.** "Check whether a DeFi product's Sharia approval still holds." plus the lede says what Mizan is, what it covers (tokens, pools, vaults) and both ways to read it (people here, contracts through the oracle). The live record card sits right under the lede, so the product is shown, not just described (`desktop/00-y0.png`).
- **The two-axis model is applied consistently.** `ConclusionText` (`components/Status.tsx:18-29`) greys out an approval and marks it "(historic)" whenever the status is not Active. The register, the modal and §2 all follow the rule, and the §2 time-lapse (`desktop/04-y3240.png` to `05-y4050.png`) shows Active turning into Expired while "Approved" stays put. That is the single most important idea, and the page teaches it well.
- **The register table reads in the right order:** record, then status, then validity, then conclusion. The validity column uses words that match each status ("Lapsed", "Suspended since", "Void since", "Superseded"). Status tags use icon plus colour plus text. On mobile the table becomes cards (`mobile/07-y5317.png`), and the search, type filter, status filter, empty state and "Showing X of Y · Clear filters" are all there (`components/Register.tsx:143-165`).
- **The record sheet is complete for a scholar:** what was covered and what was not, evidence, the three screens, standards, vote and dissent, purification, and a supersedes/superseded link (`desktop/modal.png`). The application-rule switch inside it, with "The board's conclusion does not change", is a clever way to show what the oracle does.
- **The hero routes each audience** ("Browse the register / Scholars: read the methodology / Builders: oracle spec"). The nav highlights the current section. The static (no-3D) fallback is a real document layout, not a broken page (`desktop-no3d/01-y810.png`).
- **The copy is plain, specific and consistent** in voice, and the demo disclaimers are present without being shouty.

## Problems

### High

1. **The register, the actual product, is buried.** On desktop the §3 header starts at about 4,790 px, more than 5 screens into a 9.8-screen page (`report.json`, `desktop/05-y4050.png`). Before it come two scroll-driven tracks, 240vh and 180vh (`styles/global.css:139-140`). Mobile is 13.1 screens long. The hero CTA jumps there, but anyone who simply scrolls has to sit through about 4 screens of explainer before seeing a single row.
2. **There is no section navigation below 1080 px.** `.nav-links{display:none}` at `max-width:1080px` (`styles/global.css:101`), with no menu button. Phones and tablets get one CTA and nothing else (`mobile/00-y0.png`), on a 13-screen page. At ≤420 px the "Demo data" flag is also hidden (`global.css:102`), so the one persistent "this is fictional" marker disappears on the most-shared form factor.
3. **On first view the legend pushes the records off screen.** Six legend cells sit between the filters and the first row. On desktop they take about 185 px, so only 7 rows fit (`desktop/06-y4860.png`). On mobile the legend is a full screen and the first card appears only at the bottom of `mobile/06-y4558.png`. A register should open on data, with definitions on demand: tooltips, a collapsible "What the statuses mean", or the definition shown in the sheet.
4. **The record sheet leaves out the verification data the page promises.** §5 "Verify a record yourself" says to "Fetch the full report from the record's report link" and to "Check `signature` against the board key published on this page" (`components/Builders.tsx:83-87`). The sheet has no report link, no hash, no signature and no board key (`desktop/modal-bottom.png`; `RecordModal.tsx` renders none of them). The data exists only behind the collapsed "Show the oracle response" JSON. The register footer's "Each record is signed … anchored on-chain. How to verify a record" therefore leads to steps no visitor can complete.
5. **The hero art contradicts the status-first rule.** The scale "stays level for any approved product. It tips only when the board did not approve" (`Hero.tsx:88-89`, `three/world.ts:602`). If you pick "Yield vault" (MZN-003, Expired) or have Sample Bridge D (Needs review), the scale stays level and reads as "fine", while the copy says read the status first. The biggest visual signal on the page encodes the secondary attribute. The pan labels "Evidence" and "Policy" (`world.ts:364`, `desktop-no3d/00-y0.png`) are never explained, and "Policy" is used nowhere else on the page.

### Medium

6. **Content repeats across sections, which inflates the length.** §1 "What every record states" (Scope, Analysis, Screens, Conclusion, Limits) and §4 "Method" (Intake, Evidence, Screens, Vote, Publication, Renewal) cover largely the same steps. §1 "Analysis" also repeats §4 "The board". Status definitions appear four times: the §3 legend, the §5 matrix, the sheet subtitle and §2. Merging §1 into §4, or cutting §1 to a 5-item strip, would save 2–3 screens.
7. **Nav labels do not match section headings.** "How a review works" leads to "§1 What every record states", "Status over time" to "§2 An approval has a sunset", "For builders" to "§5 For builders: the oracle" (`Nav.tsx:4-10`). §1 also promises "these five questions", but the five items are noun labels, not questions.
8. **The hero chooser hides what it actually switches.** It is labelled by product type (Token / Yield vault / Lending pool), but what it really picks is the outcome (Active / Expired / Rejected). A visitor has no reason to expect the vault to show an expired record. "Lending pool" is also called "DeFi Pool" in the register and data, and "Yield vault" becomes "Yield Vault" (`Hero.tsx:7-11` against `data/reviews.ts:5`).
9. **The status and the conclusion overlap.** "Rejected" is a review status that only restates the conclusion "Not approved". It never changes over time, which breaks "reviewStatus … is what changes over time" (`Builders.tsx:10`). The §5 rule matrix quietly drops Rejected (`Builders.tsx:47`, `.filter((s) => s !== 'Rejected')`), so a builder reading the table cannot tell how a rejected record behaves. The "No review required" column is all ticks and adds no information.
10. **Records cannot be linked.** Opening a record sets React state only, with no `#MZN-004` hash or URL (`App.tsx:23,34`). For a public register whose whole point is "check and cite this record", every record needs a shareable address, and the browser Back button should close the sheet.
11. **The scroll tracks skip content.** In 3D mode the §1 panel moves from step 1 to step 3 in one 810 px scroll (`desktop/01-y810.png` to `02-y1620.png`), so the "Analysis" step (the reviewer roles) is easy to miss entirely. On mobile the §1 and §2 ledes are hidden (`global.css:149`), and so are the §2 jump buttons (`global.css:185`). That removes the instruction "Scroll to run…" and the only non-scroll control (`mobile/04-y3038.png`).
12. **Conversion stops at the edge of the page.** No link anywhere leaves the page: no repository, API docs, contract address, contact, or "Request a review" for issuers, who are a primary audience (`grep http components/` returns nothing). "Builders: oracle spec" lands on one example request. The footer (`desktop/10-y8100.png`) has no nav and no CTA.

### Low

13. **The JSON `reportHash` line overflows its code block** and is clipped on desktop (`desktop/09-y7290.png`).
14. **The hero says "issued 2026"** (year only, `Hero.tsx:63`), while every other surface gives a full date.
15. **The demo's "today" is fixed at 29 Oct 2026** (`lib/dates.ts:4`, `checkedAt`). That is reasonable for stable counts, but nothing on the page says so, so "348 days left" and the "Today" jump silently drift from the reader's real date.
16. **On mobile the search placeholder is cut off** ("…or scop", `mobile/05-y3798.png`). The sheet's close button scrolls away with the content (`desktop/modal-bottom.png`), and there is no sticky header or footer close on a long sheet.
17. **In the static §2 the sun-arc expiry tick and the slider expiry tick do not line up** (`desktop-no3d/02-y1620.png`), so the two date markers disagree visually.

## Top 5 recommendations (ranked by score impact)

1. **Move the register up and cut the explainers.** Put §3 right after the hero, or embed a compact 3-row register preview in the hero with "See all 8 records". Fold §1 into §4 as a single "What a record contains / How it is produced" section, and shorten the scroll tracks (how: 240vh → ~140vh with 5 equal steps; time: 180vh → ~120vh). Target ≤7 screens on desktop and ≤9 on mobile.
2. **Make the register open on data.** Replace the 6-cell legend with a one-line "Status: what it means ▸" disclosure, or with tooltips/popovers on each status tag, and put the definition sentence into the sheet. Keep the status filter. On mobile, put the filters in a single row (search, then a "Filters" button).
3. **Make the sheet the verifiable record.** Add a "Provenance" block (attestor, board key, report hash with copy button, signature, anchoring block, "Download report" link). Give each record a URL (`#record/MZN-004`), add a "Copy link" action, and make Back close the sheet. Pin the close button and the status line at the top while the sheet scrolls.
4. **Make every signal follow status first.** Tie the scale to the review status: level only for Active, tipped or greyed for anything not current. Label the pans in terms the page uses, or drop the labels. Relabel the hero chooser by outcome ("Current approval · Lapsed approval · Not approved"), with the product name as the second line, and unify type names ("DeFi Pool" everywhere).
5. **Add mobile navigation and real exits for each audience.** Add a compact section menu (a sheet or a horizontal scroll strip of the 5 sections) below 1080 px, and keep "Demo data" visible at all widths. Give each audience a final action: Scholars → "Read the full methodology (PDF)"; Builders → "API reference / contract address / GitHub"; Issuers → "Request a review". Repeat them in the footer. Restore the Rejected row in the matrix (or explain its absence), and remove or footnote the all-tick column.

## What would get this to 85+

Restructure so the register is the spine of the page, with the explainers reduced to one tight, skimmable section, and the page about 30% shorter on every device. Make every record a linkable, verifiable document that shows its own provenance. Make every visual and label (the scale, the hero chooser, the matrix, the nav) obey the same "status first, conclusion second" rule the copy teaches. With working mobile navigation and a clear next step for scholars, builders and issuers, this would be a distinctive, trustworthy fintech register rather than a well-written explainer that happens to contain one.
