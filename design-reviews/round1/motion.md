# Motion and interaction review: Mizan (round 1)

**Score: 71/100.** The scene works as a metaphor: the gates are the parts of a record, the day is the review period and nightfall is expiry. The edges are rough: a visible seam, a night sky over the register, text that snaps between steps, the lit gate hidden on mobile, and no reduced-motion version of the scene.

## What works

- **The 3D scene says something.** The five arches are the five parts of a record. The active arch turns brass, and the camera passes through each arch exactly as its step ends (`world.ts:377`, `world.ts:484-486`: the camera z is `-8.5-175p` and the gates are 35 units apart). In §2, scroll moves the sun across the afternoon, the sun sets on the expiry date, and the hourglass sand follows the share of the period that has passed (`world.ts:523-528`, `setSand`). After expiry come stars, lit windows and lamp halos (`desktop/04-y3240.png` → `desktop/05-y4050.png`). The status flips at sunset while the conclusion card stays the same. That is the product's core idea, shown in the scene.
- **The scale's tilt carries meaning.** It stays level for any approved product and tips only for "Not approved" (`world.ts:374`, `PRODUCT_TILT`). The static SVG carries the same rule (`desktop-no3d/00-y0.png` caption). This is a micro-interaction that teaches the rule.
- **The render loop is careful.** It draws only on demand (`wake()` plus a `moving` check, `world.ts:558-623`), stops when the modal is open, and stops once the opaque document sections cover the canvas (`world.ts:568`). I measured **0 draw calls over 3 s of idle** at the top of the page. three.js is code-split (513 KB chunk) and loaded in `requestIdleCallback` after the content renders, and the canvas fades in (`World3D.tsx:46`, `global.css:51`). The first frame snaps into place (`k=1`), so there is no fly-in on load. If WebGL fails, the page drops cleanly to the still layout (`onFail`).
- **The fallback is a real document, not a dimmed copy of the 3D page.** §1 becomes a numbered five-part list (`desktop-no3d/01-y810.png`). §2 becomes a range slider with a sun-arc SVG, the copy changes ("Move the date…" in place of "Scroll to run…", `TimeSection.tsx:32`), and the page gets shorter (6946 vs 8848 px). The step and day buttons scroll to the matching position in 3D mode and set state directly in static mode (`useScrollModel.ts:101-116`).
- **It avoids stock "AI landing page" motion.** There are no fade-up-on-enter reveals, staggered cards, gradient blobs or parallax. The paper-cut valley, pointed arches and minaret skyline are a distinctive look, and the motion is all driven by scroll.

## Problems

- **High: A hard seam across the 3D scene between the hero and §1.** `.mode-3d .hero::before` paints the paper gradient only inside the hero box (`global.css:106`). As the hero scrolls out, its bottom edge cuts a straight horizontal line through the scene. In `scratchpad/shots-tool/m-y420.png`, at y≈480, the left half above the line is washed paper and below it the scene is at full strength. This is the most visible defect in the first 10 seconds.
- **High: The night sky stays on through the register, which contradicts the meaning set up in §2.** §2 teaches that night means expired, but the camera stays at `tod 1.15–1.32` (deep night, lit windows) behind the register (`world.ts:530-535`, `desktop/06-y4860.png`, `desktop/07-y5670.png`), and most records shown there are Active. The register shot also looks at nothing in particular, so the 3D story ends in a dark skyline with no final beat. A hard horizontal cut then ends the 3D (`desktop/07-y5670.png`, y≈355).
- **Med: The step panel snaps between steps.** In `HowSection.tsx:97-114` the `step-body` swaps its content instantly while the camera eases with a ~220 ms time constant. The two motion systems are out of step: the camera glides and the text jumps. Panel height also changes per step (`desktop/01`, `02`, `03` panel bottoms at 508/522/498 px), so the sticky card's bottom edge shifts. The brass gate fades in (`world.ts:613`) but the button highlight does not transition.
- **Med: On mobile, the sticky panel hides the scene it explains.** In `mobile/02-y1519.png` the panel says "2 Analysis", but the brass gate is hidden behind the panel at bottom right and only a dark gate is visible. `VIEW_PORTRAIT` (`world.ts:497`) shifts the subject by only 0.22 of the height, while the bottom-anchored panel (`global.css:147`) covers about 45% of the screen. On mobile the 3D is mostly decoration.
- **Med: Shadow shimmer and blocky shadows.** The sun and its shadow camera are re-centred on `cam.l` every frame (`world.ts:597-598`) with no texel snapping, so a 1024² PCF map over a 110-unit frustum shifts under the scene during scroll. In stills the shadows are visibly stair-stepped (`desktop/02-y1620.png`, the lamp-post shadow at the lower left; `desktop/04-y3240.png`, under the hourglass plinth). During scroll this shows as crawling edges.
- **Med: The hero's right half is empty while the 3D loads.** In `mode-3d` the static scale art is not rendered (`Hero.tsx:86`, `staticMode &&`), and the canvas starts at `opacity:0`. Until three.js downloads, parses and builds about 300 meshes, the hero's right half is plain paper. Under SwiftShader that took about 12 s. On a mid-range phone it is still a noticeable gap. There is no poster frame.
- **Med: Reduced motion removes the 3D entirely.** `staticMode = reduceMotion || !webgl` (`App.tsx:21`). Reduced motion asks for less motion, not no pictures. A still render per section (sunset frame, night frame) with a cross-fade-free swap would keep the main visual for those users. As it stands, desktop-reduced is identical to desktop-no3d.
- **Low: The draw-call budget is unmanaged.** I measured about 390–420 draw calls per frame (main plus shadow pass) while settling at §1. Every palm is about 10 meshes (`world.ts:243-274`), every lamp is 3, and every hill card casts and receives shadows. Static geometry is never merged. It is fine on desktop GPUs but costly on low-end mobile, where shadows stay on (`world.ts:70`, only the map size shrinks).
- **Low: Nav jumps and pausing behave badly.** The camera lerps in a straight line in world space (`world.ts:575`), not along the road, so a nav jump from the register to the top flies diagonally through hill cards and gates. If the modal opens mid-flight, the paused frame freezes partway (`desktop/modal.png` shows a hill and palm fronds, not the hero scale). `dt` is clamped to 0.05 (`world.ts:569`), so on slow frames the camera falls further behind the scroll.
- **Low: An oversized label sprite in the hero-to-§1 dolly.** The "Evidence" label sprite looms at the right edge as the camera passes the scale (`m-y420.png`). The arch numbers are hidden before 0.6·howS, but the pan labels are not.
- **Low: `canRun3D()` leaks a WebGL2 context.** It creates a context and never releases it (`lib/webgl.ts:4`), so a second context exists alongside the real renderer.

## Top 5 recommendations (ranked by score impact)

1. **Fix the seams and give the scroll path a final beat.** Move the hero's readability gradient to a fixed overlay whose opacity follows scroll (0 → 1 → 0 over the hero's last 30%), or put it on the canvas as a CSS mask, so it never shows an edge. For the register, bring the light back: animate `tod` from night to a new dawn (`tod` → 0.05–0.15) between `timeE` and `reg`. The point is "a new review restarts the clock". Point the camera at something deliberate, such as the scale seen from far down the road. Replace the hard canvas cut at `M.doc` with a 20–30vh paper-coloured gradient sheet over the canvas.
2. **Tie the panel text to the camera.** Key `step-body` by step and cross-fade it with a 4–8 px translate over 220 ms (the camera's time constant), skipped under reduced motion. Set a fixed `min-height` on `step-body` for the tallest step so the sticky card stops resizing. Add a 150 ms background transition on `.steps button[aria-pressed]`.
3. **Re-frame the scene for portrait.** Compute the view offset from the measured panel height (`--ph` already exists) so the active subject sits in the band visible above the panel: `v[1] = (ph/2 + margin)/vh`. For portrait §1, also raise the camera and tilt it down so the brass gate sits in the top 40% of the screen. Verify with screenshots at each of the five steps on 390×844.
4. **Add a poster frame and a still mode.** Pre-render a WebP of the hero station (and optionally the §2 sunset and night frames) and show it as the hero background until `#stage.ready`, then cross-fade. Reuse those stills for reduced motion. Under reduced motion, keep the canvas but snap camera and `tod` with `k=1` (no easing) and step them per section rather than per pixel, or show the stills. Fall back to the plain document only when WebGL is missing.
5. **Cut the rendering cost and the shimmer.** Merge static meshes per material with `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeGeometries` (hills, cities, palms and lamp posts in one pass), turn off `castShadow` on hill cards beyond about 60 units, and snap the shadow camera's centre to shadow-texel increments (or bake shadows, since the sun only rotates in §2). Use `PCFSoftShadowMap`, or VSM with blur, at 2048 on desktop. Turn shadows off entirely when `small`. Target under 60 draw calls per frame. Also make the camera follow the road spline between stations rather than a straight lerp.

## What would get this to 85+

The concept is already strong enough for 85+. The execution lets it down at the joins. Remove every visible seam (the hero gradient edge, the canvas cut-off) and end the scroll path on a deliberate final frame with the right meaning. Make the panel copy and the camera move as one, with matched easing and a stable card height. Make the scene readable on mobile, where the panel currently hides the subject. Finally, keep the visual for reduced motion and for slow loads, with a poster frame and still frames, and get draw calls and shadows into a mobile budget. With those fixes, the scroll story would match the quality of the idea behind it.
